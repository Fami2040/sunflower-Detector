## What the paper actually claims

- **Problem:** Few-shot **object detection** — many labeled instances on **base** classes, only *K* bounding boxes per **novel** class (generalized setting: AP on base + novel).
- **Method (TFA):** Two-stage training on **Faster R-CNN**:
  1. **Base training:** Jointly train feature extractor **F** (backbone, RPN, proposal-level FC) and box predictor on base classes only.
  2. **Few-shot fine-tuning:** Build a small balanced set (base + novel, *K* shots per class); **freeze F**; fine-tune **box classifier + regressor only**; stage-2 learning rate **÷20** vs stage 1. Optional cosine box classifier with instance-level normalization.
- **Empirical focus:** Outperforms meta-learning FSOD baselines on revised PASCAL VOC / COCO / LVIS few-shot benchmarks; stresses **evaluation variance** and multi-seed benchmark design.
- **Not claimed:** Sunflower or agricultural imagery; tray/session domain shift; YOLO; same-class adaptation without novel categories.

## Abstract (DOI / arXiv / PMLR — consistent)

Detecting rare objects from few examples; meta-learning had dominated attention. **Fine-tuning only the last layer** of existing detectors on rare classes is critical and beats meta-learning by roughly **2–20 AP points** (sometimes ~2×). Revised benchmarks on PASCAL VOC, COCO, and LVIS with multiple sampled few-shot training groups; TFA sets SOTA on those benchmarks. Code and pretrained models public.

## Suggested manuscript sentence (draft)

For adaptation to new acquisition trays, we use a **staged fine-tuning** protocol inspired by TFA (Wang et al., ICML 2020): retain weights from the pooled model, freeze early backbone stages, and fine-tune detection heads on labels from held-out trays at reduced learning rate—an operational analogue to their few-shot head-only stage, applied here to **domain shift** rather than novel categories.
