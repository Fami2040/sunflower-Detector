# Abstract

**Background.** High-throughput, reproducible measurement of developed versus aborted sunflower seeds on benchtop capitula supports yield-oriented breeding, yet manual counting is slow and inconsistent on dense heads (approximately 500 instances per image).

**Methods.** We developed HARCHOC (Helianthus annuus rapid classification heuristics of capitula): standardized tray imaging, seed-level annotation (developed versus aborted), and two-class object detection at 1280 px resolution. A counting-first protocol selects a single global confidence on the validation split by minimizing per-image count mean absolute error (MAE), then locks that threshold before any held-out test evaluation (locked confidence ≈ 0.15; export confidence 0.001, maximum detections 3000, category-aware match intersection-over-union 0.3). Detection ranking mean average precision (mAP) is reported separately from counting. Systematic comparisons include 15-epoch augmentation ablations, a 100-epoch confirmatory training run, and a model zoo spanning multiple YOLO generations and external DETR-family detectors on identical frozen splits.

**Results.** On the held-out test split (*n* = 109 images; mean ≈ 554 ground-truth boxes per image), the production detector achieved test count MAE 61.3 seeds per image (bootstrap 95% confidence interval 51.3–71.3). At the same locked operating point, mean relative counting error was 12.0% (median 9.3%); 13.8% of images had relative error below 2%. A 100-epoch augmentation confirmatory run reached MAE 64.1. Completed 100-epoch zoo retrains (YOLOv8m, YOLO11m, YOLO26m on identical splits) yielded MAE 111.9–119.6—worse than the anchor; YOLOv10m was finishing training at draft time. Test ranking mAP50 under the canonical evaluation protocol was 0.18, not the 0.793 cited in the submitted manuscript.

**Conclusions.** Imaging was performed on dried heads under controlled benchtop conditions at a single site; tray-level count error varies widely across acquisition sessions. Field lighting, fresh material, multiple varieties, and multi-site validation require additional data. A two-stage Telegram deployment is described separately from the manuscript science metrics reported here.
